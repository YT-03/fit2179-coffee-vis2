Australia's Coffee Culture

How a single beverage became central to a nation's identity, economy, and daily ritual.

A Café on Every Corner

With more than 55,000 cafés nationwide, Australia has one of the highest café densities in the developed world. But the picture isn't uniform — Tasmania and Victoria pour more cafés per person than New South Wales or Queensland, while the Northern Territory remains a coffee outlier.

A Decade of Growth

Australia's café industry has expanded by nearly 30% since 2017. The pandemic caused a sharp dip in 2020 as venues closed and consumers stayed home, but recovery was swift — by 2022 the industry had not only rebounded but accelerated past pre-COVID levels.

What's in the Cup

Australians have distinct tastes. The flat white — invented in either Sydney or Auckland, depending on who you ask — is a staple, but the latte still dominates café orders nationwide. Together, milk-based drinks make up more than 70% of all coffee sold.

Prices vary significantly by city. A flat white in Sydney costs an average of $5.50, around $1 more than the same drink in Hobart. Melbourne sits just behind Sydney, while the smaller capitals offer noticeably cheaper coffee.

The World's Café Capital

Melbourne consistently ranks first globally for cafés per capita — outranking Vienna, Rome, and Paris. The city's café culture, rooted in post-war Italian and Greek immigration, has become inseparable from its identity. London, New York, and Tokyo all trail well behind.
