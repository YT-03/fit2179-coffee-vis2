A Café on Every Corner

With more than 55,000 cafés nationwide, Australia has one of the highest café densities in the developed world. Tasmania and Victoria pour more cafés per person than New South Wales or Queensland, while the Northern Territory remains a coffee outlier.

Tasmania quietly leads the country — its remote charm draws cafés in numbers that punch well above its small population.

The Industry at a Glance

Australia's café industry has grown relentlessly — nearly 30% more cafés since 2017, and revenue more than tripled since 2004. Milk-based drinks dominate the cup, and prices vary by over a dollar depending on which city you're in.

From COVID lows to record revenues — the industry adapted, and what Australians ordered stayed remarkably consistent.

The Capital of Coffee

Melbourne consistently ranks first globally for cafés per capita, outranking Vienna, Rome, and Paris. Its café culture, rooted in post-war Italian and Greek immigration, is most densely concentrated in the CBD and inner-city zones.

No city in the world rivals Melbourne for sheer café concentration.

Who Drinks, Where It Comes From

Coffee drinking spans every generation — peaking in the 25–34 age group but strong across all ages. The morning rush dominates when people visit. And the beans themselves travel far, with Brazil, Vietnam, and Colombia supplying nearly two thirds of all Australian imports.

Coffee is a generational constant, a morning ritual, and a global supply chain — all in one cup.
