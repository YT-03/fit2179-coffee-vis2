Chapter 01 · The Nation

A nation of cafés.

Australia has more than 55,000 cafés — among the highest per-capita densities in the developed world. But the picture isn't uniform. Tasmania quietly leads on a per-person basis, while Victoria and New South Wales dominate by total count. Together, café numbers have grown by nearly 30% since 2017, and revenue has more than tripled since 2004.

Chapter 02 · The Cup

What Australians drink, and what it costs.

Milk-based drinks dominate the menu. The latte and flat white together account for more than half of all coffee sold. Prices vary by city — Sydney leads at $5.50 a cup, Hobart trails at $4.50. The beans themselves travel from six continents, but Brazil supplies almost a third. Coffee crosses generations, peaking sharply in the 25–34 working-adult age band.

Chapter 03 · The Capital

Melbourne: the world's café capital.

No city on Earth has more cafés per capita than Melbourne — not Vienna, not Rome, not Paris. Its café culture, rooted in post-war Italian and Greek immigration, has become inseparable from the city's identity. Zoom in further and the concentration becomes even more extreme: the CBD alone hosts more cafés than any other inner-city zone — and the daily rhythm peaks sharply in the morning hours.

The last sip.

From a Sydney espresso to a Carlton flat white, coffee in Australia is more than a drink — it's an industry, a ritual, and a marker of place. The data tells us where it grew, what it costs, and who's drinking it. The next decade will tell us how much further it can go.

Methodology & Sources

Every figure on this page traces to a publicly available source — primarily the Australian Bureau of Statistics, IBISWorld, Statista, YouGov Australia, the City of Melbourne's CLUE dataset, and Mordor Intelligence. Chart 09 (hourly café traffic) uses a modelled distribution based on published hospitality traffic patterns. All Vega-Lite specifications are open-source in the project repository alongside cleaned CSV data files.
