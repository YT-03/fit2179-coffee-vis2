Australia's Coffee Culture

How a single beverage became central to a nation's daily ritual.

FIT2179 Data Visualisation 2 · By Yum Tarabzoni

A Café on Every Corner

With more than 55,000 cafés nationwide, Australia has one of the highest café densities in the developed world. But the picture isn't uniform, Tasmania and Victoria pour more cafés per person than New South Wales or Queensland, while the Northern Territory remains a coffee outlier.

Tasmania quietly leads the country — its remote charm draws cafés in numbers that punch well above its small population.

A Growing Industry, A Distinctive Taste

Australia's café industry has expanded by nearly 30% since 2017. The pandemic caused a sharp dip in 2020, but recovery was swift, by 2022 the industry had not only rebounded but accelerated past pre-COVID levels. Meanwhile, milk-based drinks dominate what's in the cup: the latte and flat white together account for more than half of all coffee sold.

From a COVID low to record growth: the café industry didn't just survive the pandemic, it adapted and accelerated — and what Australians ordered stayed remarkably consistent.

The Price of a Cup, and the Capital of Coffee

Prices vary significantly by city. A flat white in Sydney costs an average of $5.50, around $1 more than the same drink in Hobart. But Melbourne tells a different story on the world stage, it consistently ranks first globally for cafés per capita, outranking Vienna, Rome, and Paris. The city's café culture, rooted in post-war Italian and Greek immigration, has become inseparable from its identity.

A flat white may cost a dollar more in Sydney than Hobart, but no city in the world rivals Melbourne for sheer café concentration.
